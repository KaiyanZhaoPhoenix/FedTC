\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We design our empirical evaluation to answer four concrete questions corresponding directly to the claims in Sections~\ref{sec:introduction} and~\ref{sec:method}:
\textbf{(Q1)} Does the robustness--heterogeneity dilemma genuinely starve honest minority clients under standard Dirichlet non-IID partitions?
\textbf{(Q2)} Can FedTC outperform static and myopic baselines across both overall accuracy and minority-class retention?
\textbf{(Q3)} Is sequential RL control ($\gamma > 0$) paired with utility cross-checking ($\lambda_n$) genuinely needed under non-stationary attacks (\textbf{Stage A})?
\textbf{(Q4)} Does our minority-preserving interval $[L_n, U_n]$ protect honest minorities under strategic feedback misreporting where median-residual filtering fails (\textbf{Stage B})?
\emph{(Note: Table entries marked ``--'' indicate pending runs in the unified evaluation suite.)}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\paragraph{Datasets, heterogeneity, and backbone.}
We evaluate on \textbf{CIFAR-10}, \textbf{CIFAR-100}, long-tailed \textbf{CIFAR-100-LT} (imbalance factor $\mathrm{IF}=20$), and \textbf{Tiny-ImageNet}. Unless stated otherwise, we partition datasets across $K = 60$ clients via symmetric Dirichlet sampling $\mathrm{Dir}(\alpha)$ with $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.1\}$, and sample $K_{\mathrm{train}} = 20$ clients per round ($C = 1/3$). Each client splits its local data 90\%/10\% into $\mathcal{D}_k^{\mathrm{train}}$ and held-out $\mathcal{V}_k$. Because standard BatchNorm running statistics are easily corrupted by severe non-IID skew and Byzantine updates, we use a \textbf{GroupNorm ResNet-18} backbone across all methods.

\paragraph{Threat models.}
We test six forward poisoning attacks at malicious ratio $\rho \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.3\}$: \textbf{ALIE}, \textbf{Min-Max}, \textbf{Min-Sum}~\cite{shejwalkar2021manipulating}, \textbf{IPM}, \textbf{Label Flipping}, and \textbf{Model-Replacement Backdoor}. Malicious clients simultaneously launch \textbf{Fake Utility} inflation ($u_b^{(t)} = 1.0$) and, when sampled in $\mathcal{E}_n$, execute three backward misreporting attacks: \textbf{All-Max Inflation} ($+M_{\mathrm{loss}}$), \textbf{Minority-Silencing Echo} (mimicking the honest majority's positive score), and \textbf{Bimodal Jamming} ($\pm M_{\mathrm{loss}}$).

\paragraph{Baselines and dual metrics.}
We compare FedTC against server-data-free defenses---FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}, RFA~\cite{pillutla2022robust}, Trimmed-Mean~\cite{yin2018byzantine}, Multi-Krum~\cite{blanchard2017machine}, Bucketing~\cite{karimireddy2021learning}, and NNM---as well as reference methods requiring a clean server root dataset (marked $\dagger$: FLTrust~\cite{cao2020fltrust}, FedAA~\cite{he2025fedaa}). To capture both global utility and minority starvation, we report \textbf{Global Test Accuracy (\%)} alongside \textbf{Minority / Tail-20\% Class Accuracy (\%)}.

\subsection{Ablation Studies: Stage A and Stage B}
\label{sec:ablation_studies}

\paragraph{Stage A: Dynamic control under non-stationary attack switching.}
To answer \textbf{(Q3)} under ideal feedback ($z_{i,n} = z_{i,n}^{\mathrm{true}}$), we run a 300-round non-stationary schedule ($H=5, N=60$ macro-steps): Clean warmup (Rounds 1--100) $\to$ ALIE (Rounds 101--200) $\to$ Min-Max (Rounds 201--300). We compare \textit{Fixed-Strict}, \textit{Fixed-Permissive}, \textit{Oracle Best Static}, \textit{Contextual Bandit} ($\gamma=0$), and \textit{FedTC Dynamic RL} ($\gamma=0.9$), alongside a $\lambda_n \equiv 0$ ablation when Fake Utility is injected at Round 150.

\paragraph{Stage B: Strategic feedback misreporting and interval containment.}
To answer \textbf{(Q4)} and validate Theorem~\ref{thm:ci_containment}, we compare four backward reward estimators under All-Max, Minority-Silencing Echo, and Bimodal Jamming: \textit{Raw Mean}, \textit{Median-Residual Filtering}, \textit{Cosine-Dispersion Heuristic}, and \textit{FedTC $[L_n, U_n]$ + Fallback Gate}. We track empirical containment $\mathbb{P}(\bar{Z}_n^\star \in [L_n, U_n])$, minority accuracy retention, and fallback activation rate.
