\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Byzantine-Robust Aggregation.}
Classical Byzantine defenses infer a consensus update purely from update geometry: Krum selects updates closest to their neighbors~\cite{blanchard2017machine}, Bulyan combines selection with coordinate-wise trimming~\cite{guerraoui2018hidden}, and Median/Trimmed-Mean apply coordinate-wise robust statistics~\cite{yin2018byzantine}. RFA further stabilizes aggregation via the geometric median~\cite{pillutla2022robust}. While effective against large outliers, static geometric rules tend to over-filter benign long-tail clients under severe non-IID skew~\cite{peng2024mean}. Subsequent work addresses this limitation through subspace filtering (DnC)~\cite{shejwalkar2021manipulating}, momentum-based centered clipping~\cite{karimireddy2021learning}, sparsification~\cite{panda2022sparsefed}, adaptive clipping~\cite{allouah2024adaptive}, and refined theoretical bounds~\cite{zhu2023byzantine,mai2024rflpa}. Unlike these open-loop rules, FedTC closes the loop with backward validation feedback so filtering strictness adapts online to both heterogeneity and non-stationary attacks.

\paragraph{Trust Signals and Adaptive RL Control.}
Several defenses incorporate auxiliary signals beyond single-round geometry. FoolsGold down-weights clients with suspiciously similar gradient directions to curb Sybil attacks~\cite{fung2018mitigating}, whereas FLTrust~\cite{cao2020fltrust} and FedAA~\cite{he2025fedaa} calibrate client weights or train RL policies using a small clean server ``root'' dataset. Utility-driven client selection methods such as Oort~\cite{lai2021oort,fu2022clientselection} rely on client-reported loss reductions, leaving them exposed to strategic inflation~\cite{gao2021fifl,xiong2023truthful,chakarov2024incentivizing}. FedTC bridges these lines of work: it audits unverifiable utility claims against verifiable geometric deviations without any server-side clean data, and replaces proxy-dataset RL rewards with a minority-preserving confidence interval.

\paragraph{Deep Reinforcement Learning.}
Value-based methods such as DQN~\cite{mnih2015human,schaul2015prioritized} and policy-gradient or actor--critic algorithms---including A3C~\cite{mnih2016asynchronous}, TRPO~\cite{schulman2015trust}, PPO~\cite{schulman2017proximal}, DDPG~\cite{silver2014deterministic,lillicrap2015continuous}, and SAC~\cite{haarnoja2018soft}---provide effective tools for continuous online control. FedTC builds on actor--critic learning over $H$-round macro-steps, pairing PopArt return standardization with dual interval critics and an explicit fallback gate to keep online adaptation safe under adversarial feedback.
