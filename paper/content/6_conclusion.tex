\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied robust federated aggregation under the robustness--heterogeneity dilemma, where static filters starve honest minority clients while zero-trust deployments deny the server both clean proxy data and trustworthy client feedback. Our proposed \textbf{FedTC} framework resolves this tension by pairing an analytically bounded forward calibration layer with a backward confidence-interval RL controller. On the forward pass, dimensionless geometric-median deviations and rank-normalized utility claims yield continuous weights with guaranteed non-collapse and an exact cutoff radius. On the backward pass, worst-case tail trimming over decoupled macro-step evaluations constructs a minority-preserving confidence interval $[L_n, U_n]$ that drives PopArt-normalized dual critics and an execution-level conservative fallback gate.

Two practical boundaries remain for future investigation. First, our backward interval relies on an upper bound $\rho_{\max} < 1/2$ on the malicious fraction and assumes honest evaluators hold a representative local validation split. Second, while the current formulation adapts global calibration parameters $(\lambda_n, \tau_n)$ across macro-steps, extending the empirical suite across full long-tail and non-stationary attack benchmarks---and exploring per-layer or personalized calibration---are natural next steps.
